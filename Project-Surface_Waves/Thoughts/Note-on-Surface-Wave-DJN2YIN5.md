### <span style="background-color: rgba(241, 152, 55, 0.5);">Stokes forcings</span>

The definition of Stokes forcing is given in <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FG9D5N4DW%22%5D%7D%2C%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FCPIHUEBF%22%5D%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/G9D5N4DW">Fujiwara et al., 2026</a></span><span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/CPIHUEBF">Suzuki and Fox-Kemper, 2016</a></span>)</span>, referring to the conservatice processes without wave dissipative processes. It includes the Stokes advection of (1) background vorticity (planetary vorticity) through the Coriolis-Stokes force (CSF); (2) the relative vorticity through the vortex force (VF) and (3) mass and tracers, and some higher-order corections depending on scaling assumptions and the complexity of the formulation.

The below sections summarised the influence of Stokes forcings in different spatial scales.

#### <span style="background-color: rgba(162, 138, 229, 0.5);">Mixed layer scale</span>

Where Stokes drift velocity can be considered horizontally uniform

*   “The Stokes forcings largely <span style="color: rgb(255, 32, 32);">alter surface boundary layer currents</span>. In mixed layer scale problems, the Stokes drift velocity can be considered horizontally uniform, and the mixed layer flows interact with the “local” Stokes drift field. The CSF is known to induce an Eulerian countercurrent that tends to cancel the Stokes drift and keep the Lagrangian transport unchanged” <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FG9D5N4DW%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%222%22%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/G9D5N4DW">Fujiwara et al., 2026, p. 2</a></span>)</span>

### <span style="background-color: rgba(241, 152, 55, 0.5);">Mechanisms</span>

*   Role of sea-spray enhalpy flux <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FCPG7KQAT%22%5D%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/CPG7KQAT">Li et al., 2026</a></span>)</span>:

    *   incorporating sea-spray effects in a TC model enahnces the modeled latent and sensible air-sea heat fluxes
    *   Additional heat fluxes contribute to stronger diabatic heating within the eyewall (a critical driver of TC secondary circulation)
    *   Diabatic heating intensifies the radial pressure gradient, accelerating boundary-layer inflow and promoting low-level and promoting low-level convergence and moisture transport
    *   sea spray and wind speed form a mutually reinforcing Wind-Spray-Wind (WSW) positive feedback loop, prowing updrafts in the eyewall, deepening the central pressure and increasing the peak intensity

*   Role of surface-roughness correction

## <span style="background-color: rgba(46, 168, 229, 0.5);">High-frequency Contribution to the Stokes drift profile</span>

## <span style="background-color: rgba(46, 168, 229, 0.5);">Langmuir Turbulence &#x26; Stokes Drift</span>

*   “Inclusion of Langmuir Turbulence and Coriolis-Stokes force requries to model the magnitude and the shear of the Stokes drift velocity correctly. Stokes drift profiles are also needed when estimating the drift of partially or entirely submerged objects <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FZAF7T989%22%5D%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/ZAF7T989">Breivik et al., 2014</a></span>)</span>

*   However, computing the Stokes drift profile is expensive since it involves evaluating an integral with the 2D wave spectrum at every desired vertical level. It is also often impractical or impossible since the 2D wave spectrum may not be available” <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FZAF7T989%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%222433%22%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/ZAF7T989">Breivik et al., 2014, p. 2433</a></span>)</span>

    *   For this reason, it has been customary to replace the full drift velocity by a monochromatic profile matched to the transport and the surface Stokes drift <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FZAF7T989%22%5D%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/ZAF7T989">Breivik et al., 2014</a></span>)</span>

    *   However, problematic: **in reality, there will be stronger shear near the surface and a stronger deep drift**

        *   shear under a broad spectrum is much stronger thna that of a monochromatic wave of intermediate wavenumber due to the presence of short waves (high-wavenumber) whose assocaited Stokes drift quickly vanishes with depth in the upper part of the column
        *   At the same time, the deep Stoke drift profile wull be stronger than that of a monochromatic wave since the long wave (low-wavenumber) components penetrate deeper

## <span style="background-color: rgba(46, 168, 229, 0.5);">Action density &#x26; Intrinsic wave frequency</span>

*   <span class="highlight" data-annotation="%7B%22attachmentURI%22%3A%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FBDF6Y77C%22%2C%22pageLabel%22%3A%227%22%2C%22position%22%3A%7B%22pageIndex%22%3A6%2C%22rects%22%3A%5B%5B287.8808249881853%2C394.36057288%2C538.6522061920153%2C404.24351176%5D%2C%5B85.08041000000014%2C382.48047288%2C538.5166396691357%2C392.36341175999996%5D%2C%5B85.08021000000019%2C370.48047288%2C208.65095000000017%2C380.60251511999996%5D%5D%7D%2C%22citationItem%22%3A%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FBGFRKLJS%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%227%22%7D%7D" ztype="zhighlight"><a href="zotero://open/library/items/BDF6Y77C?page=7">“The most elegant formulation of the “energy” balance equation is in terms of the action density spectrum N which is the energy spectrum divided by the socalled intrinsic frequency σ.”</a></span> <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FBGFRKLJS%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%227%22%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/BGFRKLJS">ECMWF, 2024, p. 7</a></span>)</span>

    *   the intrinsic frequency is the *oscillation frequency of a surface gravity wave measured in a reference frame moving with the local ocean current*.
    *   represents the natural frequency of the wave relative to the fluid
    *   Formula:  $\sigma=\sqrt{gk\tanh{(kh)}}$  and wave frequency (dispersion relationship) can therefore be described as  $\omega=\omega (k,x,t)=\sigma+\mathbf k \cdot \mathbf U$ , where the depth  $h(\mathbf x, t)$  and the current  $\mathbf U(\mathbf x,t)$  may be slowly-varying functions of  $\mathbf x$  and  $t$

*   wave groups with action N have energy $\sigma N$ and momentum $kN$ <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FBGFRKLJS%22%5D%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/BGFRKLJS">ECMWF, 2024</a></span>)</span>. The wave action $N$ is conserved without the source term, can yields the transport equation of action as:

    $$
    \frac{\partial }{\partial t}N+\frac{\partial }{\partial z_i}(\hat{z_i}N)=0
    $$
