| **<span style="color: rgb(25, 60, 71)"><span style="background-color: rgb(243, 250, 244)">Abstract: </span></span>***<span style="color: rgb(25, 60, 71)"><span style="background-color: rgb(243, 250, 244)">We discuss the coupling processes between surface gravity waves and adjacent winds and currents in turbulent boundary layers. These processes mediate exchanges of momentum, heat, and gases between the atmosphere and ocean and thus are of global significance for climate. Surface waves grow primarily by pressure-form stress from airflow over the waveforms, and they dissipate in the open sea by wave breaking that injects and stirs momentum, energy, and bubbles into the ocean. Wave motions pump wind eddies that control fluxes across the lower atmosphere. Flow separation occurs behind steep wave crests, and at high winds the crests flatten into spume, which diminishes the drag coefficient. In the ocean the Lagrangian-mean wave velocity, Stokes drift, induces a vortex force and material transport. These generate Langmuir circulations penetrating throughout the boundary layer and enhancing entrainment at the stratified interior interface in combination with other turbulent eddies and inertial-shear instability.</span></span>* |

### 💡 Background

*   The ABL and OBL connecet fluxes at the boundary and interior interface, they are distinctively characterized by high Reynolds number, stratification, and most importantly by **coupling that is through a dynamic interface shaped by gravity waves**

    *   the surface waves tat develop and connect the ABL and OBL span wavelengths ranging from milimeter to hundreds of meters, **depending on duration and magnitude of the wind forcing**
    *   the surface wave field moves randonly, propagates rapidly, supports wind stress, breaks intermittently, forces winds in the atmosphere, and induced langmuir circulations (LCs) in the ocean

*   eventhough the depth of ABL and OBL are quite modest,the boundary-layer turbulence and surface waves play a critical role at larger scales as they regulate the functioning of Earth’s climate and weather systems

    *   climate forecasts are dependent on the turbulent **exchange of momentum and scalars at the air-sea interface** and at the depth of the ocean mixed layer
    *   Wave breaking and the subsequent generation of bubbles and spray **control gas exchange**, impact stratocumulus-cloud-deck formation, alter ocean acoustics, and influence the remote sensing of sun glitter.
    *   Hurricane inttensitu and track predictions are crucially dependent on the air-sea interface drag and enthalpy coefficients and themixing efficiency of the OBL.
    *   Turbulent hurricane wakes also generate planktom blooms through the entrainment of cool fresh water at the base of the thermocline.
    *   Statistical wave-prediction models rely heavily on parameterisation of the wind-input (wave growth) and dissipation (wave breaking) terms that appear as source terms in the wave-action equation

*   Present understanding of the fundamental processes that connect winds, waves, and currents is inhibited by the challenging task of acquiring outdoor observations

    *   Platform movements, sensor techonology, cost, and broadband variability of the atmosphere, ocean and wave field all limit the acquisition of observational data sets in the marine surface layers.

*   Computational simulation of the marine boudary layers is equally challenging;

    *   Direct coupling of air and water under steady state conditions that account for different physical propertiers 9density, visvosity) is possible using turbulent closure but is onlu applicable to low-wind situations in which waves have small amplitude and do not break.
