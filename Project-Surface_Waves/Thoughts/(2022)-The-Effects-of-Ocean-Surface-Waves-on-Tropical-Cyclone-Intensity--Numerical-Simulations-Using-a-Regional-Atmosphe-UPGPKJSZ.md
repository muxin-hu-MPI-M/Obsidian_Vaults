| **<span style="color: rgb(25, 60, 71)"><span style="background-color: rgb(243, 250, 244)">Abstract: </span></span>***<span style="color: rgb(25, 60, 71)"><span style="background-color: rgb(243, 250, 244)">Tropical cyclones (TCs), especially landfalling intense storms often pose serious threats to life and property in coastal areas. Although TC track forecast skill has been improved in the past decades, the progress of advancing the intensity forecast lags that of the track forecast. One possible limiting factor is the absence of ocean surface waves in forecast systems. To better represent the interaction of TC and underlying ocean, a regional atmosphere-ocean-wave coupled model is employed in this study. Twenty-one TCs of a whole year in 2013 are retrospectively simulated through twin simulations, a Control and a Fully coupled run. Results show that TC intensity bias has been significantly reduced in the fully coupled simulation, in which five ocean surface wave related physical processes are considered, including wave modulation of momentum flux, sea spray effect on enthalpy flux, surface current and Stokes drift on air sea flux, non-breaking wave induced mixing in the upper ocean as well as rain induced ocean surface cooling. A case study approach is used to diagnose the effect of individual surface wave related physical process on TC simulations. Similar to the effect of sea spray, surface waves also act as positive feedback on TC intensification by modulating air-sea momentum flux. Absolute angular momentum budget analysis suggests that larger radial inflows and stronger updrafts near the eyewall promote the radial and vertical advections of absolute angular momentum and in turn lead to a stronger TC in Fully coupled simulation. The TC structure and size agree better with observations in Fully coupled simulation.</span></span>* |

### 💡 Background

*   Previous studies based on coupled atmosphere-ocean simulations or forecast have demonstrated the importance role of ocean coupling in TC intensity prediction; Benefitting from an interactive ocean model and data assimilation, weak TCs in ERA5 were well reproduced; But challenges still remain as operational NWP models tend to underestimate strong TCs

*   TCs draw energy from the underlying warm ocean trhough exchanging air-sea enthalpy (i.e., <span style="color: rgb(32, 33, 34)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)">the sum of a </span></span><span style="color: rgb(51, 102, 204)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_system" title="Thermodynamic system" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">thermodynamic system</a></span></span><span style="color: rgb(32, 33, 34)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)">'s </span></span><span style="color: rgb(51, 102, 204)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_energy" title="Internal energy" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">internal energy</a></span></span><span style="color: rgb(32, 33, 34)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)"> and the product of its </span></span><span style="color: rgb(51, 102, 204)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)"><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure" title="Pressure" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow">pressure</a></span></span><span style="color: rgb(32, 33, 34)"><span style="background-color: rgb(255, 255, 255)"> and </span></span><u>volume</u>) flux

*   Most of the numerical prediction reanalyses substantially underestimate the latent heat flux in hurricane conditions; While XseaFlux data performed much better due to the inclusion of surface wave related physical processes

    *   enthalpy flux differences are highly correlated with the TC intensity bias;
    *   the enthalpy flux difference increases linearly with the extent of intensity underestimation

*   <span style="background-color: rgba(255, 102, 102, 0.5)">ocean surface waves play an important tole in air-sea enthalpy flux exchange which directly affect the air-sea flux and TC intensity.</span>

    *   **Air-sea momentum flux depends on wind condition AND the sea state**

        *   With the wave state dependent air-sea momentum fux scheme, a better wind-pressure relationship was forecasted
        *   Observation shows that the drag coefficient (i.e., wind stress) tends to saturate (饱和) and levels off when the surface wind speed exceeds 33 ms-1

    *   Under TCs, **breaking wave induced sea spray could dramatically increase the contact area between the ocean the atmosphere**, leading to more upward air-sea heat and mositure flux.

    *   numerical results suggest that **TCs were strengthened by including the enthalpy effect of sea spray effect**

    *   **surface waves affect air-sea mass flux by modulating the emission of sea salt aerosol which could act as cloud condensation nuclei**.

*   Indirect effect of surface wave: regulating upper-ocean sturcture;

    *   Wave-induced mixing facilitates the negative feedback (i.e, TCs -> entrainment and the upwelling brings colder water -> cooling of surface)

### 👩🏻‍💻 Method

*   Regional atmosphere-ocean-wave coupled model: FIO-AOW framwork

    *   **Atmosphere model: WRF4.1**

        *   non-hydrostatic; wide selection of sub-grid scale param

    *   **Ocean model: POM**

        *   Mellor-Tamada turbulence closure scheme
        *   is coupled with MASNUM in an independent ocean-wave coupled forecasting system

    *   **Wave model: MASNUM**

        *   The characteristic inlaid method was applied to integrating the wave energy spectrum balance equation
        *   both regional and global in spherical coordinate

*   The ocean and the wave models restarts from the analysis of ocean-wave coupling forecast system, but with different boundary conditions

*   **C-Coupler 2** is used to achieve the two-way parallel coupling among the mdoel components;

    *   With dynamical 3D coupling: the z-coordinate and the elevation of the sea are dynamical

    *   ocean-wave coupling are not interpolated; while variable transfer between atmos to ocean and wave models is interpolated through bilinear interpolation algorithm

        *   air-sea fluxes (momentum and enthalpy) are calculated uniformly in atmosphere component by importing variables from the ocean and wave models

*   The air-sea intergace is no longer a distinct two layer structure due to the breaking of surface waves under strong winds of TC. Instead, a vapor-liquid mixed layer is formed with the air filling with sea spray droplets and the water filling with bubbles

    *   Within this layer, the wind stress tends to be saturated with the increase of wind speed -> to match the observation!!!
    *   the upward enthalpy flux is greatly enhanced

### 🔬 Innovation

*   Introduce **two additional surface wave related physical processes** in the latest version of FIO-AOW which is employed in this paper

    *   wave modulation of air-sea momentum flux
    *   sea surface current and Stokes drift on air-sea flux.

*   **<span style="background-color: rgba(255, 212, 0, 0.5)">Surface Wave Modulation of Air-Sea Momentum Flux</span>**

    *   wave-induced stress is obtained by integrating the wind-input source function along direction and angular frequency

    *   “For young surface waves, almost all of momentum flux from atmosphere are absorbed by surface waves. Only after the waves are fully developed, the residual momentum flux will be transferred to ocean.” <span class="citation" data-citation="%7B%22citationItems%22%3A%5B%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FQJ7SCFWN%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%226%22%7D%5D%2C%22properties%22%3A%7B%7D%7D" ztype="zcitation">(<span class="citation-item"><a href="zotero://select/library/items/QJ7SCFWN">Zhao et al., 2022, p. 6</a></span>)</span>

    *   therefore, the momentum flux at the air-sea interface is tau\_a (wind stress) = tau\_oc + tau\_in + tau\_diss. tau\_oc is the momentum obtained by the ocean; tau\_diss is momentum flux injected from breaking waves into the ocean. tau\_in is the wave induced stress

*   **<span style="background-color: rgba(255, 212, 0, 0.5)">Sea Spray Effect on Air-Sea Enthalpy Flux</span>**

    *   <span class="highlight" data-annotation="%7B%22attachmentURI%22%3A%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FHU45PU2C%22%2C%22annotationKey%22%3A%22M2NX7YZ6%22%2C%22color%22%3A%22%23ff6666%22%2C%22pageLabel%22%3A%226%22%2C%22position%22%3A%7B%22pageIndex%22%3A5%2C%22rects%22%3A%5B%5B166.969%2C371.85%2C570.483%2C382.182%5D%2C%5B166.969%2C359.853%2C231.525%2C370.185%5D%5D%7D%2C%22citationItem%22%3A%7B%22uris%22%3A%5B%22http%3A%2F%2Fzotero.org%2Fusers%2F13925110%2Fitems%2FQJ7SCFWN%22%5D%2C%22locator%22%3A%226%22%7D%7D" ztype="zhighlight"><a href="zotero://open/library/items/HU45PU2C?page=6&#x26;annotation=M2NX7YZ6">“The heat and moisture fluxes at air-sea interface could be divided into two parts, the interfacial and the spray modulated fluxes.”</a></span>

        *   spray-modulated flux: controlled by the interaction of droplets and their surroounding air; At high wind speed, this spray modulated flux is comparable and even larger than the turbulent fluxes

    *   Andreas et al. (2008) assumes that the total sensible and latent heat fluxes above the spray evaporation layer are simply linear sums of the spray enthalpy and interfacial fluxes
